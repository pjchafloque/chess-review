      Evaluation over the game
      What the move labels mean

      Moves are graded by expected points: your chance of winning (a draw counts half) before and after the move, worked out from Stockfish's evaluation. Losing two pawns matters a lot in an equal position and hardly at all when you're already a rook up, so the grades follow what the move does to the result, like chess.com's Game Review.

      Puzzle settings
      What counts as a puzzle

      A position from your games where exactly one move wins. Your opponent has just slipped, the right move mates or wins material, and every other move lets the chance go. If the solution takes several moves, each of your moves has to be the only good one too.

      Most blunders don't make puzzles. In the position before a blunder there were usually several ordinary moves that would have been fine, so there was nothing special to find. That's why this list is much shorter than your list of blunders.

      The rules are the ones Lichess uses to turn real games into puzzles, checked step by step with Stockfish 19. Three things are different here. Puzzles that take a single move, like taking a piece your opponent left hanging, stay in (Lichess drops them as too easy; the switch above hides them). Missed checkmates are shown as “Mate in N”, and winning material instead doesn't count, while Lichess leaves a mate out whenever another move also wins. And a chance still counts when you were already a little ahead in material. Only chances you missed in the game are here.

      Defend puzzles are the other side of it: your opponent threatens something and exactly one move holds, with every other move at least as much worse as above. Taking back a piece your opponent just captured doesn't count, since that's a reflex rather than something to find.
